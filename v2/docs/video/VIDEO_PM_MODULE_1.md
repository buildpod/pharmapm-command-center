# PM Module 1 — From plan to living command center

> **NotebookLM instruction (paste into Video Overview → Customize):**
> Audience: project managers running regulated technology implementations
> (pharma, GxP) who are NOT certified PM experts. Tone: calm, practical,
> peer-to-peer — no marketing hype, no jargon without explanation. Length:
> 6–8 minutes. Follow the narration sections of this document strictly and in
> order; do not add capabilities that are not described here.

**Goal:** a PM finishes this video able to create a project, validate what was
generated, and read the dashboard honestly — in under 15 minutes of real use.

---

## Scene 1 — The problem (0:00–0:45)

Every implementation project has three versions of the truth: the plan in one
tool, the status story told to sponsors, and the evidence scattered across
inboxes for when an auditor asks. Keeping them aligned is manual work — and
under pressure, alignment is the first thing that slips.

This command center keeps them connected by design. One set of records drives
the plan, the dashboard, and the reports. Nothing is hand-set; everything can
be traced. This module shows the first fifteen minutes: from nothing to a
living project.

## Scene 2 — Starting: the launchpad (0:45–1:45)

On first run you land on the launchpad with four honest ways in: explore a
realistic sample project, create from a pharma playbook, import an existing
plan from Microsoft Project or Excel, or start with a blank skeleton.

The playbook route matters most: pick it when your project resembles a known
rollout pattern, and the setup wizard turns a handful of project facts into a
full working structure — milestones, workstreams, tasks, risks, documents, and
budget lines.

## Scene 3 — Setup in four steps (1:45–3:15)

Setup is four steps: Discover, Choose source, Shape model, Validate.

Discover captures the facts that drive everything else — dates, regions,
governance needs. Choose source is where playbook, import, saved template, or
blank is decided. Shape model lets you tune the recommended structure — and
defer whole sections you don't want generated yet. Validate shows you exactly
what will be created before it exists.

The principle: nothing appears in your project that you didn't see coming.

## Scene 4 — Validate before you trust (3:15–4:30)

After creation the dashboard prompts a guided review: milestones first, then
task owners, then risks and documents, then charter. This matters because a
generated plan is a starting point, not the truth — the review is where it
becomes YOUR plan.

The readiness checklist on the dashboard tracks this honestly: "4 of 7 ready"
means three setup items still need attention, each one linked, each one
computed from the real records — not ticked by hand.

## Scene 5 — Reading the dashboard honestly (4:30–6:00)

The Executive Verdict is the headline: a confidence score computed from cost
efficiency, schedule pace, and forecast — never typed in by anyone. Until real
work and spend register, it says "Plan only" — because a fresh plan hasn't
earned trust; it just hasn't deviated yet.

Every number is a door: KPI cards open the milestones, risks, and cost lines
behind them. "What needs attention" turns live signals into your next actions.
If data is too thin to judge, the dashboard says so instead of inventing a
verdict.

## Scene 6 — Help is always one click (6:00–6:45)

The Guide button opens tours, page help, a glossary, and the Learn section —
short plain-language lessons on baselines, earned value, and why claimed
progress gets cross-checked. You never need a certification to run this tool;
the tool teaches the discipline as you use it.

## Scene 7 — Close (6:45–7:15)

That's the first fifteen minutes: create or import, validate what was
generated, and read a dashboard that refuses to flatter you. Module 2 covers
the weekly rhythm — what happens when a date slips, how accepted changes are
governed, and how the report writes itself from the same records.
