# Episode 0 — Why PharmaPM exists

## Opening

A project plan is a promise. Governance is the discipline of keeping that
promise honest as reality moves underneath it.

That sounds obvious. In practice it almost never happens — and understanding
why is the whole reason this tool exists.

## The three versions of the truth

On most large projects, three separate versions of the truth quietly appear.

There's the plan — the schedule the delivery team works from, edited daily.
There's the story — the status pack that goes to sponsors, assembled from the
plan plus a layer of interpretation. And there's the evidence — the approvals,
the decisions, the audit trail, scattered across inboxes and shared drives,
assembled in a panic only when someone asks for it.

Keeping those three aligned is manual work. And under delivery pressure,
alignment is always the first thing to slip. Not through dishonesty — through
gravity.

## What breaks

Once they drift apart, three things follow.

Status becomes negotiable, because the plan can be edited to match the story.
Slippage becomes invisible, because when the target moves, nothing is late.
And evidence becomes archaeology — answering "when did we know this?" takes a
week of digging.

By the time the problem is undeniable, it is expensive.

## The design response

PharmaPM is built on one structural decision: **one set of records drives
everything.**

The plan, the dashboard, and the reports all read the same records. There is no
separate status layer to maintain, so there is nothing to fall out of sync.

Three consequences follow from that, and they shape everything you'll see in
this series.

First, the financial numbers are computed, not typed. Confidence, cost
pressure, forecast — all derived from earned value. There is no field where
anyone can enter a healthier number.

Second, every change is recorded. Who changed what, when, and why — including
when a commitment date moves.

Third, every claim traces to its source. Any number on any screen opens the
records that produced it.

## Where things live

The application is organised around five areas.

**Command** is where you run the project day to day: the dashboard verdict,
Delivery Signals, reports, and the activity feed.

**Plan** holds the delivery structure: milestones, tasks, worklist, your
personal items, and readiness gates.

**Governance** holds the control record: charter, decisions, risks, issues, and
documents.

**Finance** is cost lines and budget.

**People** covers team and meetings.

Two things sit above all of them. The **Guide** button, top right, gives you
page help, a tour of whatever screen you're on, and the Learn section — short
lessons on the principles behind the design. And the **guided work** panel,
which turns your live project data into the specific next actions worth taking.

## One thing to keep in mind

It's tempting to treat this as a better Gantt chart. It isn't.

The schedule is one input among several. What the tool actually produces is a
defensible answer to a harder question: will this project land, when, at what
cost — and can you prove it?

That's what the rest of this series teaches you to operate.
