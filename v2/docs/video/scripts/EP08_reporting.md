# Episode 8 — Reporting from evidence

## The principle

A status report should be a view of the records — never a re-typing of them.

The moment a human transcribes numbers from a plan into a slide deck, two
things happen. The deck becomes a separate artefact that can drift from
reality. And it acquires an author with an interest in how it reads.

If the report is generated from the records, neither is possible.

## Why it goes wrong

You know the pattern. The pack is assembled the night before the steering
committee, from a plan that's a week stale, plus memory, plus a degree of
optimism about what will land before the meeting.

It takes hours. It's the least valuable use of a project manager's time. And the
resulting document cannot be reconciled against the working plan, because parts
of it were never in the plan at all.

## How PharmaPM implements it

Three reports, all reading the same records.

**Weekly Status** for the working level — full detail, what moved, what's
blocked.

**Steering Committee** for executives — the verdict, escalations, gate
decisions, at the altitude a sponsor actually reads.

**Workstream** for leads — tasks, dependencies, and milestones for one stream.

Each carries the governed history automatically: accepted changes from this
period, baseline changes, the integrity caveat if progress may be overstated,
and direction of travel since the last capture point.

Because they read the records, the pack and the working screens cannot tell
different stories.

## Doing it

**Command, then Reports.** Pick the audience tab.

Before you generate, press **Capture point**. This records this week's headline
snapshot — confidence and forecast go-live — so that next week the report can
show direction of travel: improving, declining, or flat.

Trend needs two points. If you never capture, you will never have a trend, and
that's the most common reason the trend section stays empty.

Then export. Print to PDF for the pack, or export to a multi-sheet Excel
workbook if finance or the PMO want the underlying data.

## What good looks like

Nothing in the pack was typed by you.

Every claim traces to a record. The accepted-changes section lists the slips you
absorbed this period, with names against them. If progress looked optimistic,
the caveat is there. If the baseline moved, it says so.

And the whole thing takes a few minutes rather than an evening.

## Common mistakes

Forgetting the capture point. It takes one click and it's the only thing
standing between you and a trend line.

Editing the exported document to soften it. The moment you do, you've recreated
the problem the whole system exists to solve — and the exported version no
longer matches the source.

And sending the Weekly Status to the steering committee because it has more
detail. It doesn't serve them. Executives need altitude and escalations; the
SteerCo report exists for exactly that reason.
