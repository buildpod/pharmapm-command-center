## Scene 1 — The problem, priced (0:00–0:40)

Regulated technology rollouts — a Veeva implementation, an SAP migration, a
validated system go-live — rarely fail loudly. They fail quietly: the plan
drifts, the status stays green, and the slip surfaces months late, when it's
most expensive. The root cause is structural: the tools these programmes run
on record intent, but cannot defend it. Every date, every percent-complete,
every RAG colour is hand-typed by someone with a stake in how it reads.

## Scene 2 — The wedge (0:40–1:40)

This command center adds the layer those tools are missing: structural
honesty. The delivery commitment is frozen — moving it requires a recorded
who, when, and why. The confidence score is computed from earned value, never
typed, and weights progress by budget so trivial tasks can't masquerade as
delivery. Claimed progress is cross-checked against the records, and the gap
is flagged in the report itself. Every number traces to its source in one
click, and every change lands in an audit log.

Excel and MS Project describe plans. Jira and its peers manage tickets.
Neither can prove anything about the delivery promise. This can.

## Scene 3 — How you buy it (1:40–2:25)

You don't start with a software rollout — you start with an audit. One
analyst takes one troubled project, imports the real plan, and runs one full
reporting cycle through the engine. The deliverable is the report: an
evidence-backed, line-by-line defensible answer to "will this land, when, and
at what cost?" — including what the current status pack isn't saying.

Your data never leaves your machines — the system runs locally, with no
vendor cloud processing, which your quality and compliance teams will
recognise as a feature.

## Scene 4 — The ask (2:25–2:55)

If you have a project where the dashboard is green but your instinct isn't:
that's the one. One project, one cycle, one report — then judge the difference
against what you're reading today.
